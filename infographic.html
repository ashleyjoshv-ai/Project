📦 The History of Internet Packets

How the internet sends your messages in small pieces.

What is a packet?

A packet is a small piece of data. When you send a photo or a message, the internet cuts it into many packets, sends them, and puts them back together at the other side.

Parts of a packet

Header Payload Trailer
Who sent it and where it is going A small piece of your message A check that nothing broke

Why use packets?

  • Many people can share the same wire at the same time.
  • If one path breaks, packets can take another path.
  • If one packet gets lost, only that piece is sent again.

Timeline

  1. 1960s Paul Baran (USA)
    Has the idea of a network that keeps working even if parts are broken.
  2. 1965 Donald Davies (UK)
    Invents the word "packet".
  3. 1969 ARPANET
    The first message is sent between two computers. It crashes after "LO", the first two letters of "LOGIN"!
  4. 1974 Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn
    Design TCP/IP, the rules that let different networks talk to each other.
  5. 1983 TCP/IP goes live
    ARPANET switches to TCP/IP on January 1st. This is often called the birth of the modern internet.
  6. Today
    Billions of packets travel the world every second. 🌍

Fun fact

Packets have a "time to live". Each time a packet passes a router, the number goes down by one. At zero, the packet is thrown away, so it never travels forever.
